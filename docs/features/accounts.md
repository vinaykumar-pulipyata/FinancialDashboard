# Accounts

Account types, levels, foreign currency, loans you owe, credit cards and how a sale of an investment is recorded.

[← Back to the README](../../README.md) · [All features](../FEATURES.txt)

- **Choosing an account and a note.** In the Income, Expense and Transfer forms the Account lists are ordered with the most used account first (by how often it has been spent from or paid into), and the Note field suggests notes you typed on earlier transactions of the same kind, most used first.

- **Accounts** of many types: cash, bank, digital wallet (PhonePe, Paytm, Amazon Pay balance: add money with a Transfer from the bank, spend with an Expense, cashback and refunds are Income; UPI payments come from the bank, not the wallet), goal fund, investment, gold, jewellery, property, vehicle, receivables, debt, credit
  card and other. There is only one Receivables and one Jewellery account, and an account can change type only within assets or within debts and never into or out of Receivables, Jewellery or Credit Card (add a new account instead). Balances are always calculated from the opening balance and the transactions, never stored.
  Debts and credit cards show the amount owed.

- **Deactivating an account and resetting its balance.** The **Edit** window of an account (the pencil on its row) has two more buttons. **Deactivate** switches the account off: it drops out of every list, no transaction can be added to it, and none already on it can be changed or deleted, and the account itself cannot be edited, until you open its Edit window again and press **Activate** (an inactive account is greyed and marked "inactive", and it is left out of net worth). **Reset balance**, only for an **active** account with money in it (or owed on it), takes its balance to zero: you type **what happened to the money** ("withdrawn in cash and closed the account"; required) and the app saves one dated transaction in the fixed category **Balance Adjustment** that carries your note, an expense when money leaves an account that holds it, an income when an overdrawn one gains it, and for a loan or card an income when something is owed (money in lowers it) or an expense when it was overpaid. History is kept, nothing is deleted, and a Balance Adjustment is not counted as spending or earning in Stats, Spending Review or Budgets. The Ledger, Mutual Funds, Shares, Recurring Deposits, Jewellery and Receivables accounts keep their balance through their own buttons and cannot be reset here.

- **Foreign currency accounts.** A Cash, Bank, Wallet, Investment, Property, Vehicle or Other account can be held in **USD, EUR, GBP or VND** (a Currency choice in the Add account popup; goal funds, Jewellery, Receivables, loans and credit cards are rupees only for now). First enter a rate in **menu > Data > Exchange rates**: how many rupees one unit is worth on a day (up to 8 decimals, so 1 VND = ₹0.0033 keeps its digits); the newest rate on or before a day is used, and there are no live prices, so add a new one when it has moved. The account shows its balance in its own currency with "about ₹…" beside it, and the net worth figures, the level totals and the net worth graph count it in rupees (the graph at the rate of each month's end). A dong amount is a whole number. You can record **income, expenses and transfers** on a foreign account: an amount is in the currency of its account, the form labels it with that symbol, and a dong amount is a whole number. **A transfer between two currencies asks for what arrived as well as what left** (for example 850 rupees out of the bank and 10 dollars into the US account): the second box is filled in from your saved rates and is yours to correct to the bank's real figure. A list row shows `received $10` under the amount, and the "remaining balance" line under a row is in the account's own currency. Loans, and paying a loan or card, stay in rupees. **Stats, Budgets, Spending Review, the Calendar totals and the search totals count a foreign income or expense in rupees at the rate of its day** (one with no saved rate is left out, and a note says so); **the Monthly and Income & Expenses sheets of the Excel export still count only rupee transactions**. The currency cannot be changed once an account has transactions.

- **Loans you owe (Level 6)** are declared as **Fixed** (the same EMI every month) or **Custom** (any amount, any
  time). A **Pay** button on each loan and card row records a payment: an expense from a bank or cash account, in the
  fixed category **Liability Payments**, that lowers the amount owed. A fixed loan starts the payment with its EMI.

- **Loans** (Debt accounts) show how much has been **repaid**: a bar and a line such as "30.0% repaid · ₹30,000 of ₹1,00,000 paid · ₹70,000 left · about 7 EMIs to go" (the whole loan is what it started with plus anything borrowed on it later; the EMIs to go are the balance over the fixed EMI, interest not counted), turning green with "Fully repaid" once nothing is owed. Under the row a **Transactions (n) ▸** fold, closed to begin with, lists the loan's payments and other transactions, newest first, in pages like the Transactions list, each with what was still owed after it.

- **Credit cards** show the **withdrawal** (what has been taken on the card and not yet paid back) and the credit still available, and any transaction that uses or pays a card shows a line under its account, "Card name remaining balance ₹X" (the limit less what the card had had withdrawn just after it; "withdrawal ₹X" when the card has no limit) (opening balance + spending and fees − payments, in date order; the Transactions list and the Calendar day both show it). A card has a limit, a usage bar and an optional **statement day** (the day the bank generates the bill) and an optional **bill due day** (the day it must be paid by), each 1 to 31 with a short month using its last day. They are two different days. The statement day is also a **monthly reminder**: "🧾 Travel Card statement" appears in the Bills panel from 7 days before (with what is outstanding so far, and on the day itself the amount of the statement: everything spent on the card minus payments **before** that day, so a transaction dated on the statement day itself belongs to the next statement), and the Calendar marks that day every month with "statement" and, once the day has come, its amount. With a statement day, the Coming up bill shows what was owed on that statement less any payments since, not the purchases made after it; without one it shows everything the card owes. **Vehicles** with number plate and year bought.

- **Credit card statement cycles.** Under each credit card's row in the Accounts view (Liabilities level) a fold, **Statement cycles ▸**, shows the card's transactions one cycle at a time, with **‹ 15 Sep – 14 Oct 2026 ›** to page between cycles (it opens on the cycle running now, ‹ goes to the one before, › to the next, and "3 of 14" says where you are); the Transactions list itself is unchanged. A cycle runs from one statement day up to the next (a transaction dated on the statement day belongs to the next cycle, as in the bill under Coming up; a card with no statement day is grouped by calendar month). Each cycle shows a status (**Open · ₹x owed so far**, **Paid**, **Part paid ₹x of ₹y**, **Bill ₹y · due 5 Nov**, **Overdue**, or **Nothing to pay**: the payments from the statement day to the due day count), its carried-in, spent and paid totals, and the cycle's transactions in the same rows as the Transactions list (each with the card's remaining balance after it), newest first, **in pages** like that list (10, 25, 50 or 100 a page, Previous / Next, "Showing 1–10 of 27"), with chips for **All**, **Spent** and **Paid into the card**.

- **Selling an investment or gold** is a transfer from that account to a bank or cash account (an (i) on the Investment and
  Gold rows and on the Transfer form says so); Mutual Funds and Shares have a Withdraw button on their cards.
