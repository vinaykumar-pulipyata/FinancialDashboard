# People, split expenses and write-offs (Receivables)

The people who owe you (or you owe), expenses shared with them, settling the balance and letting a debt go.

[← Back to the README](../../README.md) · [All features](../FEATURES.txt)

- **Loans**: people you have lent to. Lending and collecting are fixed categories (Loans Given, Loans Collected),
  each tied to a person, and Receivables is worked out from them.

- **Splitting an expense with people.** In the Expense form, tick **Split this expense with people**: you are one of the members, tick the others from the Receivables list (or **+ Add person**) and choose **Equally** (the odd paise stay with you), **By amount** or **By percent**; each share and yours are shown as you type and must add up to the bill. The transaction keeps its **full amount** (it is what left the account); what each person owes is their share, shown under their name in **Receivables** (Accounts > Receivables), where the **▸ arrow before each person's name** folds out their transactions: loans, everything shared with them and the bills they paid for you (bill, balance, settled, left, newest first), a line for what they owed before you started, any **write-off** (with its day and your reason, so you can see when you let it go; its ✏️ changes the day and reason, its 🗑 takes it back so the amount is owed again, the amount itself cannot change), and the payments made. Each person's row shows one **balance** (what they owe you, loans included, less what you owe them: "Owes you" or, as a negative amount, "You owe them"), and one **Settle** button beside their name settles that balance, not single transactions: what you owe each other is set off first (your bills against their shares, then against their loans; no money moves), then the rest is paid in the direction of the balance, so when they owe you the money comes **from them** into an account, and only when you owe more do you pay them from an account; the payments list shows what each one cleared. Stats, Budgets, Spending Review, the Calendar totals and the Transactions found-totals count a split expense at **your own share** only. An expense that is partly settled cannot be deleted or have its amount or shares changed until the payment is deleted; a person with shared expenses cannot be removed. **When someone else paid for you**, tick **Split this expense with people** and choose their name in **Paid by** (the dropdown only appears once the Split box is ticked; by default every expense is yours, paid from your account) (instead of *Me*): no account is involved and nothing leaves your accounts; enter **your share of the bill** (the transaction keeps the full bill, and Stats and Budgets count only your share). What you owe shows in their balance under their name in Receivables (same Transactions fold, same Settle button) and, as a **negative amount** (−₹X) on the person's row when you owe them more than they owe you. The Receivables total is the balance across everyone (what people owe you less what you owe them); it stays out of net worth, as before. When you owe more, **Settle** records what you paid them as a Loans Given expense that is not counted as lending. A bill that has been partly paid back or set off is locked (an offset cannot be undone). API: `paidByBorrowerId` and `ownShare` on a transaction, `POST /api/borrowers/{id}/settle-balance` (`{date, accountId, amount, note}`, what is owed each way is set off first), `settle-splits` and `repay-splits` (one direction each), `youOwe` on each person in `GET /api/loans`. Split shares are not in the Excel export or import yet (the Loans sheet has an "Of which shared expenses" column; backups keep them). API: `splits` on a transaction (`{"personId": share}`), `GET /api/splits`, `POST /api/borrowers/{id}/settle-splits`.

- **Write off**: letting go of a debt. For a loan or card you owe, the Write off button on its row takes the amount owed to zero with a note. For money a person owes you, open the pencil (Edit) on their row under Receivables and press Write off there; their owed amount and the Receivables total come down by it, the date and note are kept, and no account changes.
